# Joelle Scally (NY Fed CCP Manager) on Left-Censoring

Source: Email correspondence, March 16, 2026
**NOTE: This is ON BACKGROUND. Cannot quote or name Scally. Can use substance only, attributed to "the NY Fed" or "researchers who maintain the data."**

> "One difference between CCP and AHS will be that there is some left-censoring in the CCP. More specifically, our data begin in 1999, so if an individual had a mortgage loan open and paid it off before 1999, we will wrongly identify them as a first-time buyer if they rented from 1999-2002 and then bought in 2002. This would push up the average age, especially in the earlier years. This problem lessens as the CCP data series gets longer; e.g. the likelihood that someone owned in 1998 and then bought again in 2025 after a long stint as a renter is somewhat slim. I'm not sure about the 'ten-year window' that you mention – I think this is related though."

## Key takeaways:
- **Fed acknowledges left-censoring bias explicitly**: "we will wrongly identify them as a first-time buyer"
- **Fed acknowledges it biases upward**: "This would push up the average age, especially in the earlier years"
- **Fed acknowledges it decays over time**: "This problem lessens as the CCP data series gets longer"
- **Fed is uncertain about the 10-year credit report retention window**: "I'm not sure about the 'ten-year window'"
- **This suggests the CCP data extract may not include pre-1999 closed accounts** even if Equifax retains them — the panel snapshot starts in 1999

## Implication for our analysis:
Scally's quote validates our core thesis. However, it's unclear whether the blind spot is "everything before 1999" or "everything before ~1989" (10-year retention). Scally's uncertainty about the 10-year window, combined with her example using 1999 as the cutoff (not 1989), suggests the CCP data itself may not include pre-1999 account history even if Equifax retains it.

This is important: the 10-year retention is an Equifax *bureau-level* policy, but the NY Fed CCP is a *panel extract* that begins in 1999. The panel may only see what's on the credit file as of Q1 1999, not the full historical archive.
