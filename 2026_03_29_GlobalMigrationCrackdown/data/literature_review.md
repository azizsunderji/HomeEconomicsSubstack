# Literature Review: The Global Migration Slowdown in Mainstream Media (2024-2026)

*Compiled March 2026*

---

## Executive Summary

**Has any major publication written the definitive "immigration restriction is now global consensus across ideologies" piece?**

Not exactly. The closest candidates are:

1. **Bloomberg's Adrian Wooldridge** ("As Nations Curb Immigration, the Golden Age for Employers Is Ending," June 2025) -- the most explicitly global and cross-ideological framing, arguing the era of easy immigrant labor is ending everywhere.
2. **The Mixed Migration Centre's "Normalising the Extreme 2025"** -- the most comprehensive treatment of how restrictionist policies have gone mainstream across the political spectrum globally, though it's a think-tank report rather than a mainstream media article.
3. **The New York Times** piece on Europe closing its doors (late June 2025) -- frames the European restrictionist turn as systemic and bipartisan, explicitly noting it's happening "quietly" unlike in the US.
4. **Bloomberg's "Age of Borders" feature** (August 2025) -- explicitly frames the US, UK, and Australia as converging on restriction.

However, no single publication has written the definitive, sweeping "the entire world, left and right, has turned against immigration" article. The coverage remains largely segmented: US outlets write about Trump's crackdown; UK outlets write about Starmer; Canadian outlets write about Carney; and the OECD produces data showing the aggregate decline. The synthetic "this is a global phenomenon and it's bipartisan" argument is mostly found in think-tank reports (MPI, OECD, Mixed Migration Centre) rather than in mainstream journalism. This is an unclaimed story for a major publication.

## Key Articles by Publication

### Bloomberg

**1. "As Nations Curb Immigration, the Golden Age for Employers Is Ending"**
- **Author:** Adrian Wooldridge
- **Date:** June 27, 2025
- **Publication:** Bloomberg Opinion
- **Scope:** GLOBAL -- the most explicitly global framing in mainstream media
- **Key argument:** For as long as most people can remember, businesses have relied on ready supplies of foreign workers -- Silicon Valley, farmers, hotels, housebuilders, megastores. That era is ending. Popular discontent is rising, anti-immigrant parties are flourishing, and mainstream parties are taking note. Trump is not the only leader clamping down; Mark Carney has imposed caps on temporary workers and students; Keir Starmer has spoken of Britain as a "nation of strangers."
- **Key data:** Foreign-born population shares: UK 16%, Germany 19%, Sweden 20%, US 14.3%
- **Notable framing:** Recommends businesses prepare by investing in automation and domestic talent pipelines -- treating the immigration slowdown as structural, not cyclical
- **URL:** https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2025-06-27/as-nations-curb-immigration-the-golden-age-for-employers-is-ending

**2. "As US, UK, Australia Tighten Migration, Foreign Graduates Face Tough Job Choices"**
- **Author:** Bloomberg News (feature)
- **Date:** August 6, 2025
- **Publication:** Bloomberg (feature/magazine piece)
- **Scope:** GLOBAL -- frames US, UK, Australia as part of same restrictionist wave
- **Key argument:** Workers cannot easily go where the jobs are. The world's largest economies are raising barricades even as aging populations need workers. Focuses on Indian graduates and US scientists caught in the squeeze.
- **Notable framing:** Subtitled "How Migration and Talent Shape the Global Economy in an Age of Borders" -- the "Age of Borders" framing is significant
- **URL:** https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2025-08-06/how-migration-and-talent-shape-the-global-economy-in-an-age-of-borders

**3. "International Students Targeted in UK, Canada, Australia Immigration Crackdowns"**
- **Author:** Bloomberg News
- **Date:** August 13, 2024
- **Publication:** Bloomberg
- **Scope:** GLOBAL -- explicitly frames student visa restrictions as a coordinated global crackdown
- **Key argument:** International students face an increasingly uncertain future as governments seek easy targets to rein in surging immigration. Canada announced a 35% cut in study permits; Australia proposed a 270,000 cap; UK barred most students from bringing dependents.
- **Key data:** UK study-related visas fell from 423,000 to 266,000 in one year
- **URL:** https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-08-13/global-immigration-crackdown-ensnares-students-studying-abroad

**4. "Denmark Has a Plan to Fix Immigration and Beat the Far Right"**
- **Author:** Bloomberg Opinion
- **Date:** November 17, 2025
- **Scope:** COUNTRY-SPECIFIC but positioned as a model for the global left
- **URL:** https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2025-11-17/denmark-has-a-plan-to-fix-immigration-and-beat-the-far-right

### New York Times

**5. "Europe Is Closing the Doors: Migration Policy Tightening Is Gaining Momentum"**
- **Author:** NYT (exact author unclear from secondary sources)
- **Date:** ~June 29, 2025
- **Publication:** New York Times
- **Scope:** REGIONAL (Europe) but framed as systemic and cross-ideological
- **Key argument:** Unlike in the US, where Trump's steps have caused a storm of protests, the European "pivot" is happening quietly but is systemic and sustainable. Centrists in the EU actively support conservative approaches. Denmark's "zero" refugee policy has become a new benchmark. The EU is developing rules to send asylum seekers to third countries.
- **Key data:** Arrivals fell ~20% in the first five months of 2025; expulsions slowly rising
- **Notable framing:** Explicitly notes this is happening across the political spectrum, not just from the right

**6. "Every US Metro Area Saw Immigration Slowdown in 2025"**
- **Author:** NYT (based on Census data analysis)
- **Date:** ~March 26-27, 2026
- **Scope:** US-SPECIFIC
- **Key argument:** Immigration rates dropped in every single US metropolitan area from July 2024 to July 2025. No metro was exempt.
- **Key data:** Denver -72%, NYC -65%, Chicago -60%, El Paso -95%. Metro areas collectively added 1.7M people (down from 3.1M). NY metro went from #1 growth to #13.

**7. KFF/New York Times 2025 Survey of Immigrants**
- **Date:** 2025
- **Scope:** US-SPECIFIC
- **Key argument:** Survey of immigrant experiences amid increased enforcement, documenting fear and behavioral changes
- **URL:** https://www.kff.org/kff-nytimes-2025-survey-of-immigrants/

## Key Think-Tank / Institutional Reports

### Mixed Migration Centre

**16. "Normalising the Extreme 2025" -- Mixed Migration Review**
- **Author:** Peter Grant et al.
- **Date:** 2025 (annual report)
- **Scope:** GLOBAL -- the most comprehensive treatment
- **Key argument:** Policies once considered extreme have rapidly become mainstream. This is not gradual normalization but a sudden paradigm shift, accelerated by Trump's second presidency but embraced across the political spectrum. What the US does now may be regarded as mainstream in a few years.
- **Country examples cited:**
  - **US:** Dismantled asylum protections, suspended refugee resettlement, ICE raids in schools/churches
  - **UK (Labour):** Starmer condemned "open borders" using language critics called "far-right talking points"
  - **Germany:** Merz broke the "firewall" against AfD cooperation on immigration bills
  - **France:** Suspended birthright citizenship in Mayotte
  - **Denmark:** "Zero" refugee policy as benchmark
  - **Poland:** Authorized live ammunition against migrants; suspended asylum claims
  - **Greece:** Three-month asylum suspensions for North African arrivals
  - **Pakistan:** Deporting 800,000+ Afghan cardholders
  - **Iran:** Expelled 2 million Afghans (up from 750,000 in 2024)
  - **Dominican Republic:** 175,000 Haitian deportations in eight months
  - **Netherlands:** Criminalized providing accommodation or employment to undocumented migrants
  - **Sweden, Finland, Iceland:** Proposed citizenship revocation for serious crimes
  - **EU:** Eight member states called for "rethinking" the European Convention on Human Rights
- **Key quote:** "The World Refugee and Migration Council identified failure of 'more moderate voices to defend key democratic and human rights norms' as 'key factor' in the international refugee system's 'crumbling before our very eyes.'"
- **URL:** https://mixedmigration.org/publications/mmr/2025/normalising-the-extreme-2025/

### OECD International Migration Outlook 2025

**17. OECD Migration Outlook 2025**
- **Date:** November 2025
- **Scope:** GLOBAL (37 OECD countries)
- **Key data:**
  - Permanent migration to OECD declined 4% in 2024 to 6.2M (still 15% above 2019)
  - Declines by country from 2023-2024: New Zealand -56%, UK -41%, Canada -39%, Australia -22%, US -12%
  - Work-related migration fell 21% to ~934,000
  - International students fell 13% to 1.9M
  - "A number of OECD countries have set explicit objectives to reduce migration"
- **Countries with explicit reduction targets:** UK (May 2025 white paper), Belgium (2025 coalition agreement), Canada (temp resident threshold of 5% of population)
- **URL:** https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/international-migration-outlook-2025_ae26c893-en.html

### Brookings/AEI Joint Report

**18. "Macroeconomic Implications of Immigration Flows in 2025 and 2026"**
- **Authors:** Brookings (center-left) and AEI (center-right) -- notable bipartisan collaboration
- **Date:** January 2026 update
- **Scope:** US-SPECIFIC but notable for cross-ideological agreement
- **Key data:**
  - 2025 net migration: -295,000 to -10,000 (first negative in 50+ years)
  - 2026 projection: -925,000 to +185,000
  - GDP impact: -0.2 to -0.3 pp in 2025; -0.1 to -0.3 pp in 2026
  - Consumer spending reduced $40-60B in 2025, additional $10-40B in 2026
  - Refugees admitted: 7,600-12,000 in 2025 vs. ~105,000 in 2024
  - Breakeven job growth fell from 100K+/month (2022-24) to 20-50K (late 2025)
  - Green card issuance abroad down 20%+; border parole/NTAs from 1.41M to 67-70K
- **URL:** https://www.brookings.edu/articles/macroeconomic-implications-of-immigration-flows-in-2025-and-2026-january-2026-update/

### Migration Policy Institute

**19. "Top 10 Migration Issues of 2025"**
- **Date:** 2025
- **Scope:** GLOBAL
- **Key findings:**
  - International student enrollment in US down 17% in fall 2025
  - By mid-2025, refugee resettlement was one-third of the prior year level
  - "Governments in many high-income countries struggled to reconcile international protection obligations and labor and skills needs with public anxieties about immigration"

## The Gap in Coverage: What No One Has Written

The major publications have collectively documented every piece of the puzzle, but no single article has assembled them into a unified thesis. Specifically, no major publication has written:

1. **"The world -- left, right, and center -- has turned against immigration simultaneously."** The Wooldridge Bloomberg piece comes closest but is framed around employers, not the political consensus. The Mixed Migration Centre report is the most comprehensive but is a think-tank publication, not mainstream journalism.

2. **"This is the first time since the 1920s that the developed world has moved in unison toward restriction."** The historical parallel to the 1920s immigration acts (US, UK, Australia all restricted in that era) is sitting there unwritten. One academic paper (Hoi, 2025, in the Australian Economic Review) explicitly draws the parallel between "Trade Wars and Immigration Restrictions: Insights From the Interwar Era."

3. **"Center-left governments are now the leading edge of restriction."** Starmer, Carney, Albanese, the Danish Social Democrats -- the story of the left embracing restriction is told country by country but never synthesized. The Jacobin piece "The Left Can Win Without Immigration Restrictionism" (March 2025) is a counter-argument but implicitly acknowledges the trend.

4. **"The post-pandemic immigration surge was a brief anomaly, and the reversion is permanent."** The OECD data shows the 2022-2024 surge was historically unusual and the correction is underway, but no one has framed it as a structural regime change rather than a cyclical correction.

## Key Quotable Data Points

- **OECD permanent migration to member countries:** Down 4% in 2024 to 6.2M; work migration down 21%; student migration down 13%
- **US net migration 2025:** Between -295,000 and -10,000 -- first negative in 50+ years
- **US net migration 2026 projection:** Between -925,000 and +185,000
- **US refugee admissions:** From ~105,000 (2024) to 7,600-12,000 (2025)
- **US green cards abroad:** Down 20%+ year-over-year
- **US State Dept visas:** 250,000 fewer in first 8 months of 2025 vs. 2024
- **Canada student permits:** Down ~60%; TFW arrivals down ~50%; asylum claims down ~33%
- **UK net migration:** Down 41% in 2024; study visas from 423K to 266K
- **Australia permanent migration:** Down 22%
- **New Zealand permanent migration:** Down 56%
- **EU arrivals:** Down ~20% in first five months of 2025
- **Global refugee resettlement mid-2025:** One-third of prior year level
- **GDP cost to US:** 0.2-0.3 percentage points of growth in 2025
- **Consumer spending impact:** $50-100B reduction over 2025-2026
- **Every single US metro area** saw immigration declines in 2025
