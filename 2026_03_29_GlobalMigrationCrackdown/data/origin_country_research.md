# Global Migration Slowdown: Origin Country Research
## Compiled 2026-03-26

## 1. TOP 10 ORIGIN COUNTRIES THAT FED THE WESTERN IMMIGRATION SURGE (2022-2023)

### Tier 1: Dominant Senders

**1. India** — The single largest origin country for the global surge
- Canada: #1 source. ~149K permanent residents expected in 2024 alone; 84K in first half of 2023
- UK: #1 source. 250K long-term immigrants in YE Dec 2023 (up from much lower pre-pandemic)
- Australia: #1 source. Indian-born arrivals peaked at 111K in YE Sep 2023; +134K since 2021 Census
- New Zealand: #1 source. India registered strongest increase (+33K) in flows to NZ in 2023
- US: Major source, though flows declined 39% in 2024
- Germany: Growing source of skilled workers
- India is the world's largest emigrant population: 18.5M abroad as of 2024, up from 6.5M in 1990
- Net migration: -1.35M in 2022, -979K in 2023 (declining rate of outflow)
- ~2.5M Indians emigrate annually

**2. China** — Second-largest globally, plus high-net-worth exodus
- Australia: #2 source. +71K since 2021 Census
- Canada: #2 source
- UK: #3 source (90K in YE Dec 2023)
- New Zealand: #3 source
- Net migration: -294K in 2022, -568K in 2023 (93% increase in outflow)
- Record 15,200 millionaires left mainland China in 2024 (up from 13,800 in 2023)
- China's share of global remittances fell to 5.3% in 2024, lowest in two decades

**3. Ukraine** — Refugee-driven, sui generis
- Germany: #1 source in 2022 and 2023 (276K immigrants in 2023 alone)
- 6.2M refugees recorded across Europe as of Dec 2024
- ~40M border crossings from Ukraine recorded by Dec 2024 (not unique individuals)
- ~4.3M individuals returned to habitual residence (25% from abroad, 75% internal)
- Concentrated in Poland (~1M), Germany, Czech Republic

### Tier 2: Major Senders

**4. Nigeria** — "Japa syndrome" brain drain
- UK: #2 source (141K in YE Dec 2023), mostly health/care workers
- Canada: Rising fast — projected 21K+ permanent residents in 2024
- ~2.1M emigrated from Nigeria in 2022; 1.6M Jan-Sep 2023
- Massive healthcare drain: nurses in UK up 47% YoY to March 2023
- Doctor-to-patient ratio collapsed to 1:5,000
- UK care worker visas to Nigerians dropped 80%+ in 2024 after restrictions

**5. Philippines** — Steady OFW pipeline
- Australia: #3 source (+54K since 2021 Census)
- New Zealand: #2 source
- UK: significant source
- 2.16M OFWs in 2023, up from 1.96M in 2022; 2.19M in 2024
- Deployed 2.33M land/sea-based workers in 2023
- 74.5% go to Asia (Gulf states, etc.), 10.6% Europe
- Remittances hit record $35.6B in 2025 (but declining as % of GDP — 7.3%, lowest since 2000)

**6. Pakistan** — Record labor emigration
- UK: #4 source (83K in YE Dec 2023)
- Australia: +24K since 2021 Census
- 862K emigrated in 2023 (highest since 2015), up from 832K in 2022
- 1.67M left in 2022-2024 combined
- Mostly laborers (386K) and drivers (197K); also 8.7K engineers, 3.5K doctors

**7. Bangladesh** — Surging worker migration
- UK: significant source, particularly care workers; 42% increase in asylum seekers in 2024
- Sent record 1.3M workers abroad in 2023; 1.14M in 2022; 1.01M in 2024
- Saudi Arabia largest employer (628K Bangladeshi workers in 2024)
- Remittances: ~$30B in 2024

### Tier 3: Significant Regional Senders

**8. Nepal** — Heavy per-capita emigration
- Australia: +49K since 2021 Census (4th largest gain)
- 494K labor permits issued in 2023, up from 349K in 2022
- Net migration: -410K in 2023 (net rate of -4.4 per 1,000)
- 85%+ go to Malaysia and Gulf states
- Remittances ~$11B in 2023

**9. Syria** — Refugee/asylum-driven
- Germany: #3 source (103K asylum applications in 2023, 77K in 2024)
- Net immigration from Syria to Germany fell 25% in 2024 vs 2023
- Major OECD-wide asylum origin country

**10. Zimbabwe / Other Sub-Saharan Africa**
- UK: Zimbabwe was #5 source (36K in YE Dec 2023), mostly health/care
- UK care worker visas to Zimbabweans dropped 80%+ in 2024
- Cameroon and Eritrea entered Canada's top 10 in 2024

## 2. EVIDENCE THAT EMIGRATION FROM ORIGIN COUNTRIES HAS SLOWED

### Strong Evidence of Slowdown

**India:**
- Student visas to Canada collapsed: 9,955 permits Jan-Aug 2025 vs 149,875 in same period 2023 (93% drop)
- Canada rejection rate for Indian students hit 74% in 2025 (was 32% in 2023)
- US F-1 visas to Indians down 69% in Jun-Jul 2025 vs 2024
- UK skilled worker visas to Indians down 58% in 2024
- Indian students abroad fell 5.7% in 2025 vs 2024 (MEA data)
- India's net migration loss shrunk from -1.35M (2022) to -979K (2023) — 28% reduction in outflow rate

**Nigeria:**
- UK care worker visas to Nigerians dropped 80%+ in 2024
- UK student visas to Nigerians down 55% in 2024 vs 2023
- Driven almost entirely by destination-country policy (visa bans on dependants, higher salary thresholds)

**China:**
- Wealthy emigration may be slowing: improved business environment cited; property wealth declining reduces ability to fund moves
- China remittance share at 20-year low (5.3%) — consistent with aging population, not necessarily policy
- Student route under threat (US Republican bill to ban Chinese student visas)
- Irregular route (Mexico border) collapsed: from 20K+ crossings in 2023-24 to 80 in 2025

**Ukraine:**
- Outflow stabilized: ~6.2M refugees in Europe, but ~4.3M have returned to habitual residence
- Returns are "pendular" — not necessarily permanent

### Mixed/Weak Evidence

**Philippines:**
- OFW numbers still growing (2.19M in 2024 vs 2.16M in 2023)
- Remittances at record highs ($35.6B in 2025)
- No real slowdown — if anything, steady increase

**Pakistan:**
- 325K departed in H1 2024 — roughly on pace with 2023 record
- No clear slowdown in labor migration

**Bangladesh:**
- 2024 total (1.01M) was below 2023 record (1.3M) but still third-highest ever
- Some moderation, not a collapse

## 3. POPULATION AND ECONOMIC IMPACTS ON ORIGIN COUNTRIES

### India
- No measurable population impact: emigration of ~1-2.5M per year against a population of 1.44B (0.07-0.17%) is demographically negligible
- BUT the composition matters: disproportionately young, educated, English-speaking
- Record $129B in remittances in 2024 (14.3% of global total, highest share ever)
- If emigration slows, India retains more human capital but loses remittance growth potential

### Philippines
- Remittance-to-GDP ratio at 25-year low (7.3% in 2025) — but driven by GDP growing faster than remittances, not by reduced outflows
- Domestic economy absorbing more labor — a positive structural shift

### Nigeria
- Healthcare system devastated: 1 doctor per 5,000 patients
- 75,000+ nurses left since 2017
- UK policy changes (2024) may force some retention, but push factors (insecurity, low wages, power outages) remain extreme

### China
- Millionaire exodus represents capital flight: 15,200 HNWIs left in 2024
- Declining remittance share (5.3%) partly reflects aging population and rising domestic wages
- Fewer low-skilled emigrants as domestic economy matures

### Nepal
- Remittances ~$11B in 2023 — massive for a ~$40B economy (~28% of GDP)
- Any emigration slowdown would have outsized fiscal impact

### Bangladesh
- Remittances $30B in 2024, critical to economy
- Saudi Arabia alone absorbed 628K Bangladeshi workers in 2024

## 4. KEY FINDING: THE SLOWDOWN IS MOSTLY DEMAND-DRIVEN, NOT SUPPLY-DRIVEN

The critical insight from this research: **the reduction in immigration to Western countries is overwhelmingly driven by destination-country policy changes, not by reduced desire or ability to emigrate from origin countries.**

Evidence:
- Canada raised rejection rates from 32% to 74% for Indian students
- UK banned dependants for students and care workers; raised salary thresholds
- Australia capped student enrolments (50%+ reduction from 2023)
- US F-1 visa issuance collapsed under Trump administration policies

Meanwhile, on the supply side:
- India still has 2.5M annual emigrants and massive demand for overseas opportunities
- Philippines OFW numbers continue rising
- Pakistan and Bangladesh labor migration remains at or near record levels
- Nigeria "japa" sentiment (1 in 3 want to leave) is undiminished

**The "supply" of potential migrants from origin countries has NOT dried up. The "demand" from destination countries has been artificially restricted through policy.** This matters because it implies:
1. Migration pressure is building, not dissipating
2. If policies reverse, flows could snap back rapidly
3. Irregular/alternative routes may increase (already seen with Chinese migrants via Mexico in 2023-24)
4. Origin countries are not yet experiencing "return migration" benefits — the pipeline is being blocked at the destination end
