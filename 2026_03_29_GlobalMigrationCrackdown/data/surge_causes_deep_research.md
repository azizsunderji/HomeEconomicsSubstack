# What Caused the Synchronized Global Migration Surge of 2022-2023?

## A Comprehensive Research Document

---

## I. THE SCALE: A PHENOMENON WITHOUT PRECEDENT

The 2022-2023 migration surge was not a gradual trend. It was a synchronized, cross-country explosion that broke records in virtually every major destination simultaneously.

The OECD's International Migration Outlook 2024 documented the scale: permanent-type migration to OECD countries hit 6.5 million new permanent immigrants in 2023, a 10% year-on-year increase and 28% above 2019 pre-pandemic levels. This followed 2022, which had already set a record at roughly 6 million. More than 2.4 million work permits and authorizations were granted across OECD countries (excluding Poland), representing a 16% year-on-year increase. New asylum applications hit 2.7 million in 2023, 30% above 2022 and shattering the 2015-16 European migration crisis peak of 1.7 million.

**Source:** OECD, "International Migration Outlook 2024," November 2024.
URL: https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2024/11/international-migration-outlook-2024_c6f3e803.html

The United States remained the top destination with 1.2 million new lawful permanent residents, the highest level since 2006. The United Kingdom rose to second place with a record 750,000 new migrants (250,000 more than in 2022, a 52% increase). Germany came third with nearly 700,000. Canada followed with 470,000 new permanent migrants, and Spain recorded 360,000.

But these permanent-migration figures dramatically understate the true scale of the surge, because they exclude temporary visa holders and undocumented arrivals, which is where the most explosive growth occurred.

## II. CANADA: THE TEMPORARY RESIDENT EXPLOSION

### The Permanent Resident Ramp-Up

Canada's federal government deliberately and systematically raised immigration targets across multiple successive "Levels Plans," each one superseding the last with higher numbers.

The **2021-2023 Immigration Levels Plan** set targets of 300,000-410,000 (2021), 320,000-420,000 (2022), and 330,000-430,000 (2023) new permanent residents.

The **2022-2024 Immigration Levels Plan** raised those ranges to 360,000-445,000 (2022), 380,000-465,000 (2023), and 390,000-475,000 (2024).

Then the **2023-2025 Immigration Levels Plan**, tabled on November 29, 2022, raised them again: 410,000-505,000 (2023), 430,000-542,000 (2024), and 442,500-550,000 (2025).

**Source:** Government of Canada, "2023-2025 Multi-Year Levels Plan," November 29, 2022.
URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/transparency/committees/cimm-nov-29-2022/2023-2025-multi-year-levels-plan.html

In 2022, Canada admitted 431,645 new permanent residents, the largest number ever welcomed in a single year in Canadian history.

**Source:** IRCC Departmental Plan 2023-2024.
URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/publications-manuals/departmental-plan-2023-2024/departmental-plan.html

### The Real Story: Temporary Residents

But the permanent resident numbers were the controlled, planned portion of the system. The real explosion was in temporary residents, where there were effectively no caps.

Statistics Canada reported that Canada's population grew by more than one million people for the first time in history in 2022. Almost all of it came from immigration.

**Source:** CBC News, "Canada's population grew by record 1 million in 2022, spurred by international migration."
URL: https://cbc.ca/amp/1.6787428

Then 2023 was even bigger. Canada's population grew by 3.2%, reaching 40,769,890 as of January 1, 2024, the highest growth rate since 1957. Immigration accounted for 97.6% of population growth: 471,771 immigrants settled in the country and the number of temporary residents rose by 804,901.

**Source:** Statistics Canada, "Canada's demographic estimates for July 1, 2023: record-high population growth since 1957," September 27, 2023.
URL: https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/230927/dq230927a-eng.htm

As of July 1, 2023, an estimated 2,198,679 non-permanent residents lived in Canada, a 46% increase from the same date one year prior, the largest year-over-year increase since comparable data became available in 1971/1972. The number of non-permanent residents holding a work permit was estimated at 1,426,187, up from 868,470 a year prior, a 64% increase. The number of temporary foreign workers surged from 437,000 in 2019 to more than 1.2 million in 2023.

**Source:** Statistics Canada, "Canada's population estimates, third quarter 2023," December 19, 2023.
URL: https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/231219/dq231219c-eng.htm

### What Policies Enabled This?

Several specific policy changes opened the floodgates for temporary residents:

1. **Removal of the 20-hour work cap for students (November 2022).** Starting November 2022, the Minister of Citizenship and Immigration issued a public policy waiving the 20-hour-per-week work limit for eligible post-secondary students as a "temporary measure to fill post-pandemic labour market needs."

2. **Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) expansion.** Graduates of master's degree programs less than 2 years in length became eligible for 3-year PGWPs, up from the previous scheme where the PGWP matched the program length. This made short master's programs a highly efficient immigration pathway.

3. **Proliferation of Designated Learning Institutions (DLIs).** There was no effective quality control on which institutions could sponsor international students. Private colleges, many later exposed as "diploma mills," recruited heavily from India and other South Asian countries with misleading promises.

**Source:** Government of Canada, Canada Gazette Part 2, "Regulations Amending the Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations (Designated Learning Institutions)," November 20, 2024.
URL: https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2024/2024-11-20/html/sor-dors219-eng.html

The fraud was systemic. In 2023 alone, IRCC identified nearly 1,550 permit applications based on fake letters of acceptance. In June 2023, around 700 students from India risked deportation when it was discovered they had unknowingly obtained student visas based on forged acceptance letters doctored by immigration agents. A joint IRCC-CBSA taskforce was established to review cases; of the first 108 reviewed, 62 were determined to be genuine students.

**Source:** Government of Canada, "CIMM -- Defrauded Students and Genuine Students Impacted by Fraud Task Force & Fact Sheet," October 24, 2023.
URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/transparency/committees/cimm-oct-24-2023/task-force-students-fraud.html

**Source:** Maclean's, "How fraud artists are exploiting Canada's international education boom."
URL: https://macleans.ca/longforms/fraud-canada-education-international-students/

### The Minister's Admission

By 2024, Immigration Minister Marc Miller was forced to publicly acknowledge the scale of the problem. In a year-end interview, he described the immigration system as "overheated" and said it needed "discipline" to get the country back on track.

**Source:** Global News / Canadian Press, "Overheated immigration system needed 'discipline' infusion: minister," December 21, 2024.
URL: https://globalnews.ca/news/10929643/year-miller/

In March 2024, Miller announced a goal to reduce Canada's temporary resident population from 6.5% of the total population to 5% over three years. Over the previous two years, 60% of all newcomers had been temporary residents.

In October 2024, Miller announced the 2025-2027 Immigration Levels Plan, which for the first time ever included controlled targets for temporary residents (international students and foreign workers), not just permanent residents. The government described the plan as pausing population growth in the short term to achieve "well-managed, sustainable growth in the long term."

**Source:** Government of Canada, "Government of Canada reduces immigration," October 24, 2024.
URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/news/2024/10/government-of-canada-reduces-immigration.html

## III. UNITED KINGDOM: THE CARE WORKER AND DEPENDANT VISA EXPLOSION

### The Graduate Route (July 2021)

The UK Government introduced the Graduate Route visa in July 2021, allowing international students to remain in the UK for 2 years after graduation (3 years for PhD holders) to work in any job, with no salary threshold and no employer sponsorship requirement. This was explicitly designed to make the UK more competitive for international student recruitment following Brexit, which had ended free movement from the EU.

The impact was immediate and dramatic. Home Office analysis published May 14, 2024, documented:

- 213,250 main applicants were granted Graduate visas between July 2021 and end of 2023, plus 45,836 dependants.
- The proportion of students gaining further UK leave after studies rose from 18% in 2019 to 56% in 2023.
- 32% of all finishing students transitioned specifically to the Graduate route.
- Indian nationals accounted for 42% of all Graduate visas (89,231 grants). Nigerians were second at 11% (23,648).

**Verbatim quote:** "The top 5 nationalities account for almost three-quarters (74%) of Graduate visas issued to main applicants with Indian nationals accounting for 42%."

**Source:** Home Office, "Analysis of Migrants' Use of the Graduate Route," May 14, 2024.
URL: https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/analysis-of-migrants-use-of-the-graduate-route/analysis-of-migrants-use-of-the-graduate-route

Earnings data revealed that many Graduate route holders were working in low-paid sectors: 41% earned under 15,000 pounds annually; the median annual earnings for those working at least one month was 17,815 pounds. The top employment sector was "Administrative and Support Services" at 25% of employed graduates, followed by Health/Social Work at 16%.

### The Health and Care Worker Visa (February 2022)

This was the single biggest driver of the UK's net migration surge. On February 15, 2022, the UK government expanded the Health and Care Worker visa route to include care workers and senior care workers, who had previously been ineligible for work visas. The salary threshold was set at 20,960 pounds, far below the general Skilled Worker visa threshold.

The growth was explosive:

- Health and Care Worker visas to main applicants grew from 47,194 (year ending June 2022) to 145,823 (year ending December 2023).
- The Home Office granted roughly 350,000 Health and Care visas in 2023 in total (including dependants).
- The share of all Skilled Worker visas going to health and care roles jumped from 58% in 2022 to 75% in 2023.
- The majority of all work-related dependant visas (62%) over the three-year period were linked to the Health and Care Worker route.
- An estimated 120,000 family members of care workers received visas in 2023 alone, joining the roughly 100,000 main applicants.

**Source:** Migration Observatory, Oxford University, "Social care sector continues to drive demand for overseas workers."
URL: https://migrationobservatory.ox.ac.uk/press/social-care-sector-continues-to-drive-demand-for-overseas-workers-as-new-data-shows-public-sector-roles-dominate-work-visas/

From when the care worker route was expanded in 2022 through end of 2024, a total of 648,100 people applied for Health and Care Worker visas, including dependants.

**Source:** UK Parliament Public Accounts Committee, "Immigration: Skilled worker visas," 2025.
URL: https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5901/cmselect/cmpubacc/819/report.html

The PAC found that the Home Office "lost sight of risk of exploitation of migrant workers" when making these policy changes. There is "widespread evidence of workers suffering debt bondage, working excessive hours and exploitative conditions."

### The Dependant Explosion

Between 2019 and 2022, visas issued to student dependants increased from 16,000 to 136,000, an increase of eight and a half times. In the year ending December 2023, 102,000 dependants accompanied students (27% of total study-related arrivals), compared to just 9,000 (8%) in 2019.

Nigerian and Indian nationals dominated: Nigerians brought 67,516 dependants in the year ending June 2023 (more than double the 31,791 in the prior year), with a ratio of 1.25 dependants for every one student. Indians brought 43,552 dependants in the same period.

**Source:** ICEF Monitor, "UK visa numbers reflect surging international enrolment in 2022/23," December 2022.
URL: https://monitor.icef.com/2022/12/uk-visa-numbers-reflect-surging-international-enrolment-in-2022-23/

**Source:** Migration Observatory, "Student Migration to the UK."
URL: https://migrationobservatory.ox.ac.uk/resources/briefings/student-migration-to-the-uk/

### Net Migration Headline Numbers

The ONS estimated UK net migration peaked at 764,000 in calendar year 2022 (a figure revised upward from an initial 606,000 estimate, then 745,000). This compares to 184,000 in 2019 before the pandemic. Net migration remained above 900,000 at its peak in the year ending March 2023.

**Source:** Migration Observatory, "Net migration to the UK," updated December 18, 2025.
URL: https://migrationobservatory.ox.ac.uk/resources/briefings/long-term-international-migration-flows-to-and-from-the-uk/
Authors: Madeleine Sumption, Ben Brindle, Peter William Walsh.

### The "62% Transition" Statistic

The claim that a large proportion of student arrivals transitioned to other visas has a specific source. According to the ONS: 62% of non-EU+ visa holders on a study-related visa who arrived in the year ending June 2023 transitioned to a different visa type within two years of arrival, up from 17% for arrivals in the year ending June 2020.

An earlier ONS release (May 2024) documented the same trend with slightly different cohorts: 40% of those who arrived in the year ending December 2021 transitioned within two years, compared with just 5% of those who arrived in the year ending December 2018.

**Source:** ONS, "Reason for international migration, international students update," February 27, 2026.
URL: https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/populationandmigration/internationalmigration/articles/reasonforinternationalmigrationinternationalstudentsupdate/latest

**Source:** ONS, "Reason for international migration, international students update," May 23, 2024.
URL: https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/populationandmigration/internationalmigration/articles/reasonforinternationalmigrationinternationalstudentsupdate/may2024

## IV. AUSTRALIA: THE POST-PANDEMIC REBOUND

### Record Net Overseas Migration

Australia reopened its borders to most travelers on February 21, 2022, after nearly two years of some of the world's strictest COVID-19 border controls. What followed was a record migration surge.

In the year ending June 2023, net overseas migration contributed a gain of 518,000 people to Australia's population, the largest on record. In the year ending September 2023, the figure peaked at 556,000. In 2022-23, 737,000 migrants arrived in Australia, with three out of every four arrivals (554,000) on temporary visas. More than half of the temporary visa holders (283,000) were international students.

**Source:** Australian Bureau of Statistics, "Record high net overseas migration driven by temporary visa holders in 2022-23."
URL: https://www.abs.gov.au/media-centre/media-releases/record-high-net-overseas-migration-driven-temporary-visa-holders-2022-23

**Source:** ABS, "Overseas Migration, 2022-23 financial year."
URL: https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/population/overseas-migration/2022-23-financial-year

The top five countries of birth contributing to net overseas migration were India, China, the Philippines, the United Kingdom, and Australia (returning citizens).

### Specific Policy Changes

**1. Migration program increase (2022-23).** The government increased the permanent migration program from 160,000 to 195,000 places. Within that, the number of skilled visa places nearly doubled, from 79,600 to 142,400.

**Source:** Fragomen, "Australia: Migration Program Planning Levels Announced for 2022-2023."
URL: https://www.fragomen.com/insights/australia-migration-program-planning-levels-announced-for-2022-2023.html

**2. Pathway to permanent residency for all Temporary Skill Shortage (subclass 482) visa holders.** The government announced that by the end of 2023, all TSS visa holders would have a pathway to permanent residency.

**3. Fast-track processing for healthcare and education.** Skilled visa applications for teachers and healthcare workers were assessed in as little as three days.

**4. Working Holiday Maker expansion.** Caps were increased by 30% for the 2022-23 program year. The 6-month work limitation with one employer was suspended from January 19, 2022, through June 30, 2023, allowing backpackers to work with any one employer for the full duration of their visa.

**5. TSMIT increase.** From July 1, 2023, the Temporary Skilled Migration Income Threshold was increased from $53,900 to $70,000.

### The Parkinson Review: "So Badly Broken"

In March 2023, the government received the Review of the Migration System Final Report, led by former Treasury Secretary Dr. Martin Parkinson. The review found the system was "so badly broken" it required a "10-year rebuild" and concluded that Australia risked becoming "a guest worker society like Germany or Switzerland." The review found that temporary visa numbers had doubled over 15 years.

Home Affairs Minister Clare O'Neil stated that the TSMIT had become "frozen" and failed to track wage growth. Associate Professor Anna Boucher of the University of Sydney wrote that the system had "caused harm to Australia and to migrants, and undermined community confidence."

**Source:** University of Sydney, "Australia's immigration system set for overhaul after damning review," April 28, 2023. Author: Anna Boucher.
URL: https://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2023/04/28/australia-s-immigration-system-set-for-overhaul-after-damning-re.html

## V. UNITED STATES: ACTIVE PROGRAMS AND PASSIVE NON-ENFORCEMENT

The US migration surge was qualitatively different from Canada, the UK, and Australia. In those countries, the surge was primarily driven by legal visa channels that were deliberately widened. In the US, the surge operated through a complex mix of formal policy changes, humanitarian programs, visa backlog processing, and a massive increase in undocumented border crossings.

### Border Encounters

CBP recorded more than 2.4 million encounters at the Southwest border in FY2023 (October 2022 -- September 2023) and more than 3.2 million encounters nationwide. Between February 2021 and October 2023, total southern border encounters reached 6.5 million, including 5.8 million apprehensions between ports of entry and over 700,000 arrivals at ports of entry without authorization.

**Source:** CBP, "CBP Enforcement Statistics Fiscal Year 2023."
URL: https://www.cbp.gov/newsroom/stats/cbp-enforcement-statistics-fy2023

**Source:** FactCheck.org, "Breaking Down the Immigration Figures," by Lori Robertson, February 27, 2024.
URL: https://www.factcheck.org/2024/02/breaking-down-the-immigration-figures/

### The CHNV Parole Programs

The most significant active policy mechanism was the CHNV humanitarian parole program. Originally launched for Venezuelans in October 2022, it was expanded in January 2023 to include Cubans, Haitians, and Nicaraguans. The program offered two-year authorization to enter the US and work permits, provided applicants had not previously entered illegally and passed vetting measures.

The program operated as an explicit "carrot and stick": 30,000 monthly parole slots across all four nationalities, paired with expanded Title 42 expulsions for those who crossed irregularly. Approximately 102,000 people were paroled into the US from Cuba, Haiti, Nicaragua, and Venezuela from October 2022 through April 2023 alone. By late 2023, over 200,000 migrants had entered through the program.

**Source:** CBS News, "Biden policy that has allowed 200,000 migrants to enter the U.S. in 10 months faces key legal test."
URL: https://www.cbsnews.com/news/immigration-parole-policy-biden-migrants-court-texas/

### Title 42 and Its End

Title 42, the COVID-era public health order allowing rapid expulsions at the border, remained in effect from March 2020 through May 11, 2023. During that period, Border Patrol carried out 2.8 million expulsions. Its ending on May 11, 2023, removed the mechanism for rapid removals, though the Biden administration paired it with new transit ban rules.

### TPS Expansions

The Biden administration dramatically expanded Temporary Protected Status designations. Since 2021, DHS added Afghanistan, Cameroon, Ethiopia, Myanmar, Ukraine, and Venezuela to the TPS list, while extending protections for nine additional countries. The total list reached 16 countries: Afghanistan, Cameroon, El Salvador, Ethiopia, Haiti, Honduras, Myanmar, Nepal, Nicaragua, Somalia, South Sudan, Sudan, Syria, Ukraine, Venezuela, and Yemen.

In September 2023, the administration redesignated Venezuela for TPS with a broader eligible population.

**Source:** Pew Research Center, "How Temporary Protected Status has expanded under the Biden administration," March 29, 2024.
URL: https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/03/29/how-temporary-protected-status-has-expanded-under-the-biden-administration/

### USCIS Backlog Processing

A separate but significant contributor was the clearing of the massive visa backlog that had accumulated during COVID and the Trump era. In FY2023, USCIS completed more than 10 million immigration cases, reducing the backlog by over 760,000 cases (15%), from more than 5 million to 4.3 million. This was the first backlog reduction in over a decade.

Naturalization processing times fell from 10.5 months to 6.1 months. USCIS completed more than 1 million citizenship cases and issued more than 192,000 employment-based immigrant visas, far above pre-pandemic levels, ensuring no visas went unused for the second year running.

**Verbatim:** USCIS described this as "Completing an Unprecedented 10 Million Immigration Cases in Fiscal Year 2023."

**Source:** USCIS, "Completing an Unprecedented 10 Million Immigration Cases in Fiscal Year 2023, USCIS Reduced Its Backlog for the First Time in Over a Decade."
URL: https://www.uscis.gov/EOY2023

### The CBO Revision

The Congressional Budget Office projected that 3.3 million people on net immigrated to the United States in both 2023 and 2024, approximately 2 million more per year than previous estimates, mostly driven by higher estimates of undocumented immigrants. For context, in 2019 the CBO had projected net immigration in 2023 would total just 1.0 million.

**Source:** CBO, "Effects of the Surge in Immigration on the Federal Budget and the Economy."
URL: https://www.cbo.gov/publication/60569

## VI. GERMANY: UKRAINE PLUS DELIBERATE LIBERALIZATION

### Ukrainian Refugees under Temporary Protection

On February 24, 2022, Russia invaded Ukraine. The EU activated the Temporary Protection Directive for the first time in its history on March 4, 2022, giving Ukrainian refugees immediate residency and work rights across the EU without requiring individual asylum procedures.

Germany was the largest EU recipient after Poland. By November 8, 2022, 1,025,000 Ukrainian refugees had been registered in Germany's Central Register of Foreigners. The growth was staggering in the early weeks: 37,786 by March 6, 2022; nearly 147,000 by March 14; almost 239,000 by March 23. Germany has issued 1,547,430 decisions granting temporary protection (22.4% of the EU total). Across the EU, around 4 million people received temporary protection, with more than 3 million in the first half of 2022 alone.

**Source:** German Federal Ministry of the Interior (BMI), "Three years of Russia's war of aggression: 1.25 million refugees from Ukraine in Germany."
URL: https://www.bmi.bund.de/SharedDocs/pressemitteilungen/EN/2025/02/ukraine.html

**Source:** European Commission, "Temporary protection -- Migration and Home Affairs."
URL: https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/migration-and-asylum/asylum-eu/temporary-protection_en

### The Skilled Immigration Act (Fachkrafteeinwanderungsgesetz) 2023

Separately from the humanitarian response, Germany pursued a deliberate liberalization of labor immigration. The new Skilled Workers Immigration Act was published in the Federal Law Gazette on August 18, 2023, with provisions phased in from November 2023 through June 2024.

Key changes:

1. **Lowered EU Blue Card salary thresholds.** For shortage occupations, the threshold dropped to 39,682.80 euros; for other careers, to 43,800 euros.

2. **Decoupled work from specific qualifications.** A skilled worker with vocational training could now be granted a permit to perform "any type of skilled labor," rather than being restricted to their specific field.

3. **Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte, June 2024).** A points-based system allowing third-country nationals to enter Germany to seek employment, based on qualifications, language skills, work experience, age, and connections to Germany.

4. **Doubled Western Balkans quota.** The quota for workers from Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Kosovo, Montenegro, North Macedonia, and Serbia was doubled from 25,000 to 50,000 and made permanent.

In the first year, approximately 200,000 visas for gainful employment were issued, a 10% increase over the prior year.

**Source:** German Federal Ministry of the Interior (BMI), "The new Skilled Immigration Act one year on: numbers of foreign skilled workers, students and trainees rise considerably," November 2024.
URL: https://www.bmi.bund.de/SharedDocs/pressemitteilungen/EN/2024/11/fachkraefteeinwanderung.html

**Source:** Library of Congress, "Germany: New 'Skilled Workers Immigration Act' Enacted," August 24, 2023.
URL: https://www.loc.gov/item/global-legal-monitor/2023-08-24/germany-new-skilled-workers-immigration-act-enacted/

## VII. THE SUPPLY SIDE: INDIA AS THE ENGINE

India was the single most important source country for the 2022-2023 migration surge across multiple destinations simultaneously. This was not coincidental. A massive supply-side pressure was building.

### The Graduate Unemployment Crisis

CMIE (Centre for Monitoring Indian Economy) data paints a stark picture of educated unemployment in India:

- Youth (ages 15-24) unemployment reached 45.4% in 2022-23, six times the overall Indian unemployment rate of 7.5%.
- This had been climbing steadily: 22.17% (2017-18), 32.22% (2018-19), 38.09% (2019-20), 41.32% (2020-21), 43.53% (2021-22), 45.42% (2022-23).
- Youth labor force participation rates declined from 29.38% (2016-17) to 18.15% (2022-23), meaning even fewer young Indians were attempting to enter the labor force.

**Source:** CMIE Economic Outlook, "Youth unemployment shockingly high," by Natasha Somayya K, September 26, 2023.
URL: https://www.cmie.com/kommon/bin/sr.php?kall=warticle&dt=20230926184023&msec=816

The unemployment rate among graduates was roughly 29.1% in 2022, nine times greater than for persons who cannot read and write (3.4%). Later CMIE data (December 2023) showed graduate unemployment at 17.31%.

**Source:** CMIE, "Graduates face high unemployment rates," July 11, 2023.
URL: https://cmie.com/kommon/bin/sr.php?kall=warticle&dt=20230711182001&msec=666

By comparison, the graduate unemployment rate was approximately 2.3% in the US and 4.2% in Canada. This differential created enormous migration pressure.

### The Scale of Indian Student Outflow

According to World Bank researchers Dr. S. Irudaya Rajan and Rakesh Ranjan Kumar (September 14, 2023):

- 750,000 Indian students migrated abroad in 2022, up from 440,000 in 2021 (a 70% increase) and 250,000 in the pandemic year of 2020.
- The total number of Indian students studying abroad reached 13.2 lakh (1.32 million).
- Top destinations in 2022: USA (190,000, up from 125,000 in 2021), Canada (185,000, nearly doubled from 102,000), UK (132,000, up from 77,000 and just 25,000 in 2018), Australia (59,000).

**Source:** World Bank blog, "India's Great Student Out-Migration," by Dr. S. Irudaya Rajan and Rakesh Ranjan Kumar, September 14, 2023.
URL: https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/peoplemove/indias-great-student-out-migration-0

Indian nationals dominated immigration statistics in every major destination:
- **UK:** 42% of all Graduate visas (89,231 grants). Indian students arriving increased from 13,100 (2018) to 142,200 (2022).
- **Canada:** Indians were the single largest source of international students.
- **Australia:** India was the top country of birth for net overseas migration in 2022-23.

## VIII. THE LABOR MARKET DEMAND PULL

The synchronized nature of the migration surge was not solely a supply-side phenomenon. Destination countries experienced genuinely severe labor shortages in 2021-2022, which created both economic demand and political cover for opening immigration channels.

### The Immigration Shortfall (Peri and Zaiour, 2022)

Giovanni Peri and Reem Zaiour of UC Davis published foundational research documenting the COVID-era immigration deficit. Published January 11, 2022, on EconoFact:

**Verbatim quote:** "By the end of 2021 there were about 2 million fewer working-age immigrants living in the United States than there would have been if the pre-2020 immigration trend had continued unchanged."

Of these missing immigrants, approximately one million possessed college degrees, representing 1.8% of all college-educated workers employed in the US in 2019. Immigrant visas had decreased 45% in FY2020 compared to the previous year; nonimmigrant visas fell 54%. A backlog of over 460,000 unprocessed visa applications existed as of late 2021.

Critically, sectors with 10% greater dependence on foreign workers showed 3% higher unfilled job rates in 2021.

**Source:** EconoFact, "Labor Shortages and the Immigration Shortfall," by Giovanni Peri and Reem Zaiour, January 11, 2022.
URL: https://econofact.org/labor-shortages-and-the-immigration-shortfall

### San Francisco Fed: Immigration's Role in Labor Market Tightness

In February 2023, Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco economist Evgeniya Duzhak published research quantifying immigration's impact:

- Declining immigration between 2017-2021 contributed a 5.5 percentage point increase in the vacancy-to-unemployment (V-U) ratio.
- The 2022 rebound in immigration contributed approximately a 6 percentage point reduction in the V-U ratio.
- Between 2018-2019, foreign-born population growth slowed to 0.45% yearly, down from 1.6% annually during 2010-2016. During 2020-2021, there was a "nearly complete immigration halt."

**Source:** Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco, "The Role of Immigration in U.S. Labor Market Tightness," Economic Letter 2023-06, by Evgeniya A. Duzhak, February 27, 2023.
URL: https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2023/02/role-of-immigration-in-us-labor-market-tightness/

A follow-up July 2024 paper by Duzhak found that "about one-fifth of the V-U decline was associated with higher immigration" in 2023. She cited the CBO estimate that "3.3 million people on net immigrated to the United States in both 2023 and 2024," with an additional 1.3 million undocumented immigrants entering between October 2023 and January 2024.

**Source:** Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco, "Recent Spike in Immigration and Easing Labor Markets," Economic Letter 2024-19, by Evgeniya Duzhak, July 15, 2024.
URL: https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2024/07/recent-spike-in-immigration-and-easing-labor-markets/

### Kansas City Fed: Immigration Cooled an Overheated Labor Market

Economist Elior Cohen at the Kansas City Fed (May 2024) documented the specific labor market channels:

- The number of immigrant workers increased by around 2.5 million in 2022 and 1.5 million in 2023.
- From December 2021 to December 2023, every 1.0 percentage point increase in employment growth from immigrant workers corresponded to a nearly 0.5 percentage point decline in job vacancy rates.
- Sectors such as construction and manufacturing saw the sharpest deceleration in wage growth from 2021 to 2023.

**Source:** Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City, "Rising Immigration Has Helped Cool an Overheated Labor Market," Economic Bulletin, by Elior Cohen, May 2024.
URL: https://www.kansascityfed.org/research/economic-bulletin/rising-immigration-has-helped-cool-an-overheated-labor-market/

### OECD: Structural Shortages Across Sectors

The OECD documented that labor shortages were concentrated in contact-intensive, low-pay, and low-quality jobs, and were particularly acute in Australia, Canada, and the United States. Construction, healthcare, ICT, and accommodation/food sectors were hardest hit. After an unprecedented increase in 2022, temporary labor migration continued to grow, with more than 2.4 million work permits granted in OECD countries, 28% above pre-COVID levels.

**Source:** OECD/CEPR, "The post-COVID rise in labour shortages across OECD countries."
URL: https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/post-covid-rise-labour-shortages-across-oecd-countries

## IX. STUDENT MIGRATION AS A BACKDOOR TO THE LABOR MARKET

A recurring pattern across Canada, the UK, and Australia was the transformation of student visas into de facto labor migration pathways. This was not accidental; it was structural.

### UK: The Transition Pipeline

ONS data shows the proportion of students transitioning to non-study visas exploded after the Graduate Route was introduced:

- Year ending December 2018 arrivals: 5% transitioned to another visa within two years; 7% within three years.
- Year ending December 2021 arrivals: 40% transitioned within two years.
- Year ending June 2023 arrivals: 62% (292,000 people) transitioned to a different visa type within two years.

Work visa transitions within one year of arrival went from 1% (1,700 people) for the 2018 cohort to 11% (30,100 people) for the 2021 cohort.

Of those whose Graduate visas expired by end 2023, 63% switched to other routes: 33% to Skilled Worker, 9% to Health and Care Worker, 7% returned to study, 6% switched to family routes.

The result was a system where 1-year taught master's programs became a highly efficient immigration pathway: arrive, study for 12 months, switch to a 2-year Graduate visa, then transition to a Skilled Worker or Health and Care visa. Sixty-nine percent of Graduate visa holders had studied one year or less before transitioning.

**Source:** Home Office, "Analysis of Migrants' Use of the Graduate Route," May 14, 2024.
URL: https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/analysis-of-migrants-use-of-the-graduate-route/analysis-of-migrants-use-of-the-graduate-route

### Canada: Work Permits Swamp Study Permits

In Canada, the pathway was even more transparent. The removal of the 20-hour work limit for students (November 2022) effectively turned study permits into work permits. The expansion of Post-Graduation Work Permits meant that a 1-year master's program yielded a 3-year open work permit. The proliferation of private DLIs with little educational merit created low-cost entry points.

The result: the number of non-permanent residents holding work permits in Canada went from 868,470 (July 2022) to 1,426,187 (July 2023), a 64% increase in one year.

### Australia: The "Two-Step" System

The Parkinson Review explicitly identified Australia's reliance on "two-step" migration, where temporary visa holders (especially students) transition to permanent residency. The review found that temporary visa numbers had doubled over 15 years, with temporary migrants experiencing visa "hops" before, or without ever, achieving permanent residency.

## X. SOCIAL MEDIA AS A MIGRATION ACCELERANT

A novel factor in the 2022-2023 surge was the role of social media platforms in disseminating migration route information, reducing information asymmetries, and creating network effects.

### The Darien Gap and Chinese Migrants

The most dramatic example was the surge in Chinese migrants crossing the Darien Gap between Colombia and Panama. In 2023, more than 520,000 people crossed the gap (up from 250,000 in 2022 and just 24,000 in 2019). Of these, approximately 25,000 were Chinese, making them the fourth largest nationality and the largest from outside the Americas.

Joshua Peng of the Wilson Center documented the role of social media (March 20, 2024):

**Verbatim quote:** "Posts on Weixin (WeChat), Douyin (TikTok), and Telegram taught Chinese viewers how to apply for passports, where to fly, how to pack, and how to navigate around cartels and border officials."

A single TikTok post showing smiling migrants during the crossing was "viewed more than 13 million times." Reuters conducted more than two dozen Mandarin-language interviews at the US-Mexico border and found that "every respondent credited social media and private internet groups for how they learned about this new route to the US."

The cultural term "Runxue" (combining "run" and "study") became popular during Shanghai's 2022 lockdown, referring to the practice of leaving China. Migration costs ranged from $5,000-$7,000 self-guided to $35,000 with smugglers.

**Source:** Wilson Center, "Through the Darien Gap: A New Path for Chinese Asylum Seekers to the United States," by Joshua Peng, March 20, 2024.
URL: https://www.wilsoncenter.org/article/through-darien-gap-new-path-chinese-asylum-seekers-united-states

### IOM Research on Smuggling Networks

The International Organization for Migration (IOM) published a February 2023 study documenting how migrant smugglers use social media and instant messaging to promote and provide services. Social media sites such as Facebook, TikTok, and YouTube are used to advertise services and routes, with communication then moved to WhatsApp, Viber, Telegram, and Messenger.

The IOM noted that smugglers "have learned how to leverage these platforms better than many official organizations, providing information that is not necessarily accurate but is far more comprehensible than the information international organizations and governments provide."

**Source:** IOM, "Social Media Facilitates Migrant Smuggling in Mexico, Central America, and Dominican Republic," February 2023.
URL: https://www.iom.int/news/iom-social-media-facilitates-migrant-smuggling-mexico-central-america-and-dominican-republic

In one specific case documented by PBS, the summer of 2023 saw a rapid increase in migrants arriving from Mauritania, from almost none in previous years to over 8,000 in just over a year, after word spread on TikTok about a new route through Nicaragua.

**Source:** PBS NewsHour, "How social media lures migrants into undertaking treacherous journeys."
URL: https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/how-social-media-lures-migrants-into-undertaking-treacherous-journeys

## XI. THE CHINA FACTOR: ZERO-COVID AS A PUSH

China contributed a distinct push factor that was unrelated to labor market dynamics. Xi Jinping's Zero-COVID policy, which imposed rolling lockdowns across major cities, generated a wave of emigration interest.

Key events documented by the Wilson Center:
- December 2021: Xi'an (13 million residents) shut down; residents forbidden to exit homes.
- April 2022: Shanghai (26+ million residents) locked down. Residents reported food shortages, pets beaten to death, unannounced forced home sanitization.
- November 2022: Nationwide protests with rare open defiance. Shanghai crowds chanted: "Step down, Xi Jinping! Step down, Communist Party!" Chengdu crowds chanted: "We don't want lifelong rulers. We don't want emperors!"
- December 2022: Zero-COVID policy ended, accelerated by protests.

Immigration expert Zheng Cunzhu stated: "Zero-COVID policies severely harmed ordinary Chinese people... many people didn't care about politics before, but the pandemic awakened them. They realized there's really no freedom in China."

China's Q2 2023 GDP growth was just 0.8%. Youth unemployment hit 21.3% in June 2023 before the government stopped publishing the statistic.

In the US, 22,187 Chinese nationals were arrested entering from Mexico in the first 9 months of 2023, a 13x increase from the same period in 2022. Chinese asylum approval rates were 73.7% nationally and 87.2% in New York City, compared to the 48.5% average across all applications.

**Source:** Wilson Center, "Through the Darien Gap," by Joshua Peng, March 20, 2024.
URL: https://www.wilsoncenter.org/article/through-darien-gap-new-path-chinese-asylum-seekers-united-states

## XII. THE UKRAINE DISPLACEMENT

The Russian invasion of Ukraine on February 24, 2022, triggered the largest displacement event in Europe since World War II and was a major contributor to migration statistics in 2022-2023, though it was qualitatively distinct from the labor/student migration surges.

The EU activated the Temporary Protection Directive for the first time in its history on March 4, 2022, just 8 days after the invasion. Around 4 million people received protection, with more than 3 million in the first half of 2022 alone.

Germany received the most after Poland: 1,025,000 by November 2022, with 1.25 million as of early 2025. The UK operated the Ukraine Family Scheme and Homes for Ukraine sponsorship scheme. OECD-wide, approximately 4.7 million displaced Ukrainians were in OECD countries as of June 2023.

**Source:** European Commission, "EU Solidarity with Ukraine: one year of temporary protection."
URL: https://enlargement.ec.europa.eu/news/eu-solidarity-ukraine-one-year-temporary-protection-people-fleeing-russian-aggression-against-2023-03-09_en

## XIII. SYNTHESIS: WHY DID IT ALL HAPPEN AT ONCE?

The synchronization of the 2022-2023 migration surge across so many countries simultaneously was the product of several reinforcing factors:

**1. The post-pandemic snapback.** Every major destination country closed or severely restricted borders during COVID-19 (2020-2021). When borders reopened, there was an enormous backlog of deferred migration: students who had delayed enrollment, workers who had postponed relocating, family reunification cases that had been frozen. The Peri/Zaiour research documented a 2-million-person immigration shortfall in the US alone by end of 2021.

**2. Acute labor shortages.** The post-pandemic economic recovery created labor demand that outstripped domestic labor supply in every OECD country simultaneously, particularly in healthcare, social care, construction, hospitality, and agriculture. This created both economic demand for foreign workers and political cover for liberalizing immigration rules.

**3. Deliberate policy liberalization.** Each major destination country made specific, identifiable policy changes that widened immigration channels, often dramatically:
- Canada raised permanent targets and left temporary channels effectively uncapped.
- The UK created the Graduate Route and expanded the Health and Care Worker visa.
- Australia increased its migration program by 22% and offered pathways to permanence for temporary workers.
- Germany enacted the Fachkrafteeinwanderungsgesetz and doubled Western Balkans quotas.
- The US launched CHNV parole programs and expanded TPS designations.

**4. Supply-side pressure from origin countries.** India's educated youth unemployment crisis (45.4% youth unemployment, graduate rates many multiples of the illiterate) created an enormous pool of motivated, qualified, financially capable migrants. China's Zero-COVID policies and economic slowdown created a separate push. Venezuela's ongoing economic collapse drove hemisphere-wide displacement.

**5. The student-to-worker pipeline.** Canada, the UK, and Australia simultaneously operated systems where student visas functioned as de facto labor migration pathways, with post-study work rights creating strong pull factors and generating far more temporary residents than permanent migration targets would suggest.

**6. Social media and information networks.** Digital platforms reduced information costs and created network effects that accelerated migration decisions, from TikTok videos of the Darien Gap to WhatsApp groups sharing Canadian study permit advice.

**7. The Ukraine displacement.** The largest forced displacement event in decades added millions to European migration statistics, though this was distinct from the labor/student migration channels.

The result was not a single cause but a system of reinforcing pressures: supply-side push from origin countries met demand-side pull from destination labor markets, channeled through deliberately widened policy pathways, amplified by information networks, and supercharged by the post-pandemic rebound. Each factor alone would have produced a modest increase. Together, they produced a synchronized surge without historical precedent.
