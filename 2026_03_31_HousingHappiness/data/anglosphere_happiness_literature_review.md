# Anglosphere Happiness Decline: Literature Review

## The Core Phenomenon

The World Happiness Report 2026 documents that under-25s in the US, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand (the "NANZ" countries) rank 122-133 out of 136 countries for happiness *changes*. Life evaluations for this group have fallen by an average of 0.86 points on the 0-10 Cantril ladder over the past 20 years. For the second consecutive year, no English-speaking country ranks in the top 10 happiest nations.

The key academic paper establishing this pattern is **Twenge & Blanchflower, "Declining Life Satisfaction and Happiness Among Young Adults in Six English-speaking Countries" (NBER Working Paper 33490, February 2025)**. Using 11 datasets across the US, UK, Canada, Australia, Ireland, and New Zealand, they show the traditional U-shaped happiness curve by age has vanished -- replaced by what Blanchflower calls "a ski slope" where happiness now rises monotonically with age.

## EXPLANATION 1: SOCIAL MEDIA AND SMARTPHONES

**The dominant hypothesis.** The WHR 2026 devotes its special chapter to this topic.

**Key proponents:**
- **Jonathan Haidt** ("The Anxious Generation," March 2024) argues a "Great Rewiring" of childhood occurred between 2010-2015 as smartphones and social media became ubiquitous among teens. He cites anxiety increasing 134% and depression 106% from 2010-2018.
- **Jean Twenge** (author of "iGen" and "Generations") has argued that childhood spent with low in-person interaction and heavy online time produces depression, pessimism, and dissatisfaction across many domains.

**WHR 2026 findings on social media:**
- Across 47 countries, life satisfaction is highest at minimal social media use and declines at high levels, especially for girls and in English-speaking countries.
- When school belonging goes from low to high (10th to 90th percentile), life satisfaction gains for girls in UK/Ireland are **4x greater** than the gains from reducing social media use from high to low. Globally, the belonging effect is **6x larger**.

**The Latin America paradox:** Latin American countries have the highest rates of heavy social media use (24.2% use 5+ hours/day) yet maintain high youth wellbeing -- undermining simple causal claims about social media.

**Key skeptics:**
- **Candice Odgers** (Nature review, March 2024): "The book's repeated suggestion that digital technologies are rewiring our children's brains and causing an epidemic of mental illness is not supported by science."
- **Andrew Przybylski & Amy Orben** (Oxford, 2019): Found technology use explains at most 0.4% of adolescent wellbeing variance.

## EXPLANATION 2: HOUSING UNAFFORDABILITY

**Key source: John Burn-Murdoch, Financial Times (July 25, 2025):** Real house prices since 1995 rose 85% in the US, but 200%+ in the UK, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand, and Canada. Homeownership rates among 25-34 year-olds slumped by 20-50 percentage points.

**But the housing explanation has problems:**
- **The US doesn't fit.** The US has experienced far lower house price inflation (85%) than the UK or Canada (200%+), yet shows the typical Anglosphere unhappiness pattern.
- **UnHerd (July 27, 2025):** Argues the UK's high rate of single-parent households distinguishes Anglosphere countries more than housing costs do.
- **Twenge/Haidt's rebuttal:** Depression increased *more* among higher-income teens (151%) than among teens below the poverty line (99%). If housing/economics were the driver, the pattern should be reversed.

---

## EXPLANATION 3: LONELINESS AND SOCIAL CAPITAL DECLINE

- **Robert Putnam** ("Bowling Alone"): Documented decades of civic participation collapse. In a 2025 Fortune profile, Putnam apologized to Gen Z: "You didn't cause the problem, we caused this problem."
- **WHR 2025:** 19% of young adults worldwide reported having no one they could count on for social support -- a 39% increase compared to 2006.
- **Arthur Brooks (The Atlantic, May 2025):** "Declines in faith, family, friendship, and community are leading to greater unhappiness."

---

## EXPLANATION 4: FAMILY STRUCTURE AND DISINTEGRATION

**UnHerd (July 27, 2025):** The US has the world's highest rate of children in single-parent homes. In the UK, 44% of children born at the start of the century were not in a nuclear family for their full childhood, compared to 21% of children born in 1970.

---

## EXPLANATION 5: POLITICAL POLARIZATION AND DECLINING INSTITUTIONAL TRUST

**WHR 2025 special chapter:** Declining happiness and social trust explain "a large share of the rise in political polarisation and votes against the system." Trust that "most people can be trusted" dropped from 50% (1970s) to 30% today.

---

## EXPLANATION 6: PERSONALITY CHANGES

**John Burn-Murdoch (FT, August 2025):** Young Americans' conscientiousness plummeted from 46 to 28 since 2014, while neuroticism surged from 60 to 70. May be more *symptom* than *cause*.

---

## EXPLANATION 7: ULTRA-PROCESSED FOOD

**Sapien Labs (2024-2025):** 44% of young adults (18-34) consume UPF most days, vs. 11% of people over 55. Effects are independent of exercise and income. Core Anglosphere countries scored lowest on mental wellbeing.

---

## KEY TAKEAWAY FOR THE PIECE

1. **The US anomaly:** 85% real house price growth vs. 200%+ in other Anglosphere countries, yet shows the same unhappiness pattern -- strongest argument against housing as primary driver.
2. **The income gradient is wrong:** Depression should increase most among the poor if economics/housing were the driver. Twenge shows the opposite.
3. **The competing explanations are not mutually exclusive.** Social media/smartphones, declining social capital, loss of belonging, and economic hopelessness all likely contribute.
4. **Housing may matter not because rent is high but because unaffordability signals a broken social contract** -- young people have lost faith that hard work leads to security, and housing is the most visible symbol.
