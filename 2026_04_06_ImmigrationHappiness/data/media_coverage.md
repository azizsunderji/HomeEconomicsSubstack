# Media Coverage Survey: Immigration and National Happiness

## Coverage Gap (one sentence)
Mainstream outlets overwhelmingly attribute the happiness decline in anglophone countries to social media and youth mental health, while almost entirely ignoring the striking correlation between high-immigration countries and falling life satisfaction -- the WHR 2026 itself omits immigration as an explanatory variable despite the pattern being visible in its own data.

## Key Pattern in the Data
The WHR 2026 shows that among under-25s in the US, Canada, Australia, UK, Ireland, and New Zealand, life satisfaction scores have fallen -- placing all six among the biggest declines globally. In the US, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand, happiness for under-25s fell by an average of 0.86 points on a 0-10 scale over the past 20 years. Canada's youth ranking collapsed from near the top to 58th. Meanwhile, youth happiness stayed steady or rose in Latin America, East Asia, Poland, and other Eastern European countries with low immigration. The official WHR report attributes this to social media; immigration is not tested or discussed as a variable.

## Academic/Research Coverage

### WHR 2018 Chapter on Migration (Helliwell et al.)
- [Chapter PDF](https://files.worldhappiness.report/WHR18_Ch02.pdf)
- **Key finding:** 0.96 correlation between immigrant and locally-born happiness across 117 countries. Immigrant wellbeing converges toward host country levels. Finland ranked highest for both native and immigrant happiness.
- **Conclusion:** Immigration itself does not diminish host population wellbeing; both populations reflect broader societal/institutional quality.

### WHR 2025 Chapter: Trusting Others (Populism chapter)
- [Link](https://www.worldhappiness.report/ed/2025/trusting-others-how-unhappiness-and-social-distrust-explain-populism/)
- **Key finding:** Low life satisfaction is highly related to distrust in institutions and voting for anti-system candidates. Sharp drop in US social trust is consistent with rising unhappiness shifting voters toward populist movements. (Paywalled; could not extract full text.)

### Brockmann & Fernandez-Urbano (2025/2026) -- "Females Welcome!"
- [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167487025000868)
- **Key finding:** Female immigrants are positively associated with increased native life satisfaction; male immigrants are associated with reduced native life satisfaction. Pattern holds for both male and female natives. During economic crises, the disparity diminishes.
- **Methodology:** Fixed-effects panel models, regression discontinuity, difference-in-differences using longitudinal data from Germany and Catalonia.

### Williamson Kramer & Sanchez-Pinol Yulee (2025) -- "Trust Decay?"
- [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165176525002526)
- **Key finding:** No evidence that immigration is associated with lower trust in institutions. Positive correlation between immigration and generalized trust. Results robust to propensity score matching.

### Albarosa & Elsner (IZA) -- German refugee study
- [IZA](https://newsroom.iza.org/en/archive/research/does-immigration-undermine-social-cohesion-in-the-receiving-country/)
- **Key finding:** One million refugees in 2015-16 "did not change how Germans view their society." Effects on trust, fairness, crime concerns were "small and statistically insignificant." However: increased anti-immigrant violence in high-inflow areas for ~2 years; reduced charitable giving in high-AfD areas.

### Putnam (2007) and critiques
- Original thesis: Diversity pushes people toward "hunkering down" -- reduced trust, solidarity.
- Abascal & Baldassarri (2015) reanalysis: Diversity negatively associated with trust ONLY for white respondents, not for any other ethnic group.
- Oxford Migration Observatory conclusion: "Income inequality, in particular deprivation and impoverishment" rather than diversity itself may be the decisive factor.

### Oxford WHR 2026 Press Release
- [Link](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2026-03-19-world-happiness-report-2026-shows-complex-global-picture-social-media-and-happiness)
- **Notable:** Immigration is not mentioned anywhere. Decline attributed entirely to social media.
- **Quote:** Jan-Emmanuel De Neve (Director, Wellbeing Research Centre): "Heavy usage is associated with much lower wellbeing, but those deliberately off social media also appear to be missing out."

### Blanchflower (Dartmouth, 2025)
- [Link](https://home.dartmouth.edu/news/2025/01/expert-happiness-uncovers-worrying-trend)
- **Key finding:** Happiness decline among young people began ~2013 in US, UK, Canada, Australia, NZ, Scandinavia, and Western Europe. Attributes it to smartphones/social media, not immigration.
- **Quote:** "We're faced with a situation globally where there's been a collapse in the well-being of young people, and especially young women."

## Coverage Gap Analysis (Expanded)

**What mainstream coverage missed:**

1. **The immigration variable is never tested.** The WHR 2026 data shows a striking pattern: the six countries with the steepest youth happiness declines (US, Canada, Australia, UK, Ireland, NZ) are all high-immigration anglophone nations. Countries with low immigration (Poland, Eastern Europe, East Asia) maintained or improved youth happiness. Yet the WHR itself attributes the decline solely to social media, and every mainstream outlet that covered it -- WaPo, Bloomberg, the Oxford press release -- repeats this attribution without questioning it.

2. **Silos between beats.** Immigration is covered by politics/policy reporters; happiness is covered by wellness/lifestyle reporters. No outlet systematically connected the two. The Globe and Mail's Canada happiness article does not mention immigration despite Canada having cut immigration in direct response to public pressure about housing and jobs -- arguably the biggest policy story in Canada in 2024-25.

3. **The Canada case study is ignored.** Canada's happiness ranking collapsed from 6th to 25th overall and from near the top to 58th for youth. Canada also experienced record immigration (1.2M+ temporary residents at peak), record youth unemployment (14.7%), and a housing affordability crisis directly linked to population growth. This is the most data-rich natural experiment available, yet no mainstream outlet connects these dots.

4. **Academic research is more nuanced than coverage suggests.** The Brockmann & Fernandez-Urbano study (2025) finding that male immigration reduces native happiness while female immigration increases it is a genuinely novel result with policy implications -- and received zero mainstream coverage. The Putnam reanalysis showing diversity-trust effects only hold for white respondents (Abascal & Baldassarri 2015) is similarly absent from popular coverage.

5. **The framing is binary.** Outlets either (a) ignore the immigration-happiness link entirely (NYT, WaPo, Guardian, FT) or (b) make blunt causal claims about "open borders" causing misery (Breitbart). There is no serious, data-driven mainstream reporting that tests immigration as one variable among several (social media, housing costs, wage stagnation, institutional trust decline) in explaining the anglosphere happiness recession.

6. **Confounding acknowledged nowhere.** Social media use is high in South Korea, Japan, and much of East Asia too -- yet youth happiness has not declined there. If social media were the primary cause, we would expect declines globally, not concentrated in anglophone high-immigration countries. No mainstream outlet raises this counterargument.
