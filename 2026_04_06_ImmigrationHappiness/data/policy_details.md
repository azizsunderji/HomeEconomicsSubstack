# Immigration Policy Changes 2024-2025: Five English-Speaking Countries

## Research date: April 6, 2026

---

## 1. CANADA

### Policy Changes

**October 24, 2024 -- 2025-2027 Immigration Levels Plan announced by Minister Marc Miller:**
- Permanent resident admissions cut 21%: from ~500,000 (2024 target) to 395,000 (2025), 380,000 (2026), 365,000 (2027)
- First time Canada has reduced permanent immigration targets in decades
- Total temporary residents to fall from 673,650 (2025) to 385,000 (2026) -- a 43% year-over-year decrease
- Target: reduce temporary residents from ~6.5% to 5% of total population over 3 years

**Earlier 2024 measures:**
- January 2024: Cap on international student study permits introduced
- 2025 student permit cap reduced a further 10% relative to 2024
- March 2024: Announced plan to decrease total temporary residents to 5% of population within 3 years

### Official Statements Linking Immigration to Wellbeing/Social Cohesion

Minister Marc Miller (Oct 2024):
- "The pressures on housing and social services require a more sustainable approach to welcoming newcomers."
- Stated the plan would "support continued robust GDP growth and enable GDP per capita growth to accelerate throughout 2025 to 2027, as well as improve housing affordability and lower the unemployment rate."
- "The volume of migration has contributed to affordability [issues], but there's some nuance there."
- Claimed rent in Vancouver and Toronto had decreased since the government reduced study permits by 43%
- Housing supply gap expected to decrease by ~670,000 units by end of 2027 due to reduced immigration

**IRCC Tracking Survey (2024):** 54% of respondents said too many immigrants are coming to Canada -- citing lack of housing and lack of infrastructure/social services. This represents possibly the biggest shift in Canadian public opinion on immigration in a generation.

**Canada's 2025 Annual Report to Parliament on Immigration** frames the new plan as shifting "from volume to value -- building a system where immigration supports the nation's infrastructure, economy, and long-term social cohesion."

### Key Numbers
- Canada's population grew by 1.27 million in 2023 (3.2% growth rate), the fastest in the G7
- Net international migration: ~1.2 million in 2023, projected to drop to ~150,000 by 2027
- International students: ~1 million in Canada at peak (2023)

## 2. UNITED KINGDOM

### Policy Changes

**2024 (Conservative government):**
- December 2023 "five-point plan" to reduce immigration, measures taking effect throughout 2024
- April 11, 2024: Spouse/partner visa minimum income raised to GBP 29,000
- Social care workers barred from bringing dependants (partners/children) on their visa
- Salary threshold for skilled workers raised from GBP 26,200 to GBP 38,700

**May 2025 -- Labour government White Paper: "Restoring Control over the Immigration System":**
- July 22, 2025: Reduced list of jobs eligible for Skilled Worker visa sponsorship
- July 22, 2025: Ended overseas recruitment of social care workers
- November 2025: Settlement waiting period doubled from 5 to 10 years for permanent residence
- January 8, 2026: New B2 English requirement for Skilled Worker, Scale-up, and High Potential Individual visas
- December 2025: Immigration skills charge increased
- Graduate visa reduced from 2-3 years to 18 months
- Skilled Worker threshold raised back to RQF 6 (degree-level)

### Official Statements on Social Cohesion

**UK White Paper (May 2025) -- direct quotes:**
- "Where the pace of migration is too fast, or where integration is too weak, it can make it harder to maintain community bonds and relationships."
- "During the last Parliament, the previous Government lost control not just of the number of people arriving, but of the entire immigration system, with serious consequences for public confidence, the working of our economy, public services, the housing market and community cohesion."
- "If people want to come to Britain to start a new life, they must contribute, learn our language and integrate." (PM Keir Starmer foreword)
- "Those unsustainably high levels of migration have also added extra demand to our public services and our housing supply... fragmenting social relations."
- The White Paper frames reforms as necessary to "restore public confidence, ease pressure on public services and strengthen border control."

### Key Numbers
- Net migration peaked at 906,000 (year ending June 2023) -- "a four-fold increase in under four years"
- Net migration in year ending June 2024: 728,000
- Net migration in year ending June 2025: ~200,000 (two-thirds lower than previous year)
- Historical baseline (2010-2019): 200,000-300,000 annually

## 3. AUSTRALIA

### Policy Changes

**2024-2025 Budget measures:**
- Permanent Migration Program cap reduced from 190,000 to 185,000 for 2024-25
- 2025-26 cap maintained at 185,000
- Student visa fee more than doubled: from AUD 710 to AUD 1,600
- 9 separate changes to student migration rules in one year
- ESOS Amendment Bill 2024 (international student enrollment caps) failed in Senate, November 28, 2024

**Net Overseas Migration trajectory:**
- 528,000 in 2022-23 (record)
- Projected to drop to 260,000 in 2024-25 (decrease of 110,000 from July 2024)

### Official Statements

The Australian government's 2024 Migration Strategy committed to "simplifying the immigration system" while acknowledging that rapid population growth had placed pressure on housing and infrastructure.

**Scanlon Foundation Mapping Social Cohesion 2024 report** (Australia's most authoritative social cohesion survey):
- Social cohesion index score: 78 (same as 2023, but lowest since survey began in 2007)
- 51% of respondents say immigration is "too high" -- highest ever recorded since 2007
  - Up from 33% in 2023 (an 18-point jump in one year)
  - Up from 41% in 2019
- 85% still agree multiculturalism is good for Australia
- 71% of those who say immigration is too high still support multiculturalism
- The concern is "strongly related to economic and housing issues rather than attitudes to diversity, multiculturalism and migrants"

## 4. NEW ZEALAND

### Policy Changes

**2024-2025:**
- Net migration gain collapsed from 135,700 (2023 record) to just 11,900 (year to October 2025)
- March 2025: Work experience requirement for Accredited Employer Work Visa reduced from 3 years to 2 years relevant experience
- Median wage threshold removed; employers must now pay market rate
- Government strategy: "further tightening the Accredited Employer Work Visa policy favouring New Zealand citizens and residents"
- September 2025: New residence pathways announced (Skilled Work Experience and Trades & Technician) to launch August 2026

### Official Statements

The government framed immigration reforms as "positioning New Zealand as an attractive destination for skilled workers who can drive economic growth" while prioritizing domestic workers.

### Key Numbers
- NZ happiness ranking fell from 8th (2018) to 11th (2025) in World Happiness Report

## 5. UNITED STATES

### Policy Changes

**2024-2025 (Trump administration, inaugurated January 2025):**
- Executive orders on border enforcement, deportation acceleration
- Immigrant population declined from 53.3 million (January 2025) to 51.9 million (June 2025) due to deportations, voluntary departures, and fewer arrivals
- Border wall expansion continued
- Asylum restrictions tightened

### Key Numbers
- US happiness ranking fell from 16th (2021) to 23rd (2025) in World Happiness Report
- Immigration named "most important problem" for three consecutive months in early 2024 (Gallup)

## WORLD HAPPINESS REPORT: Immigration-Relevant Findings

### WHR 2026 (released March 2026)
The 2026 report does NOT contain a dedicated immigration chapter (the theme is social media/digital life). However, the underlying data reveals a striking pattern:

**English-speaking country happiness decline (2018-2025 rankings):**

| Country | 2018 Rank | 2025 Rank | Change |
|---------|-----------|-----------|--------|
| Canada | 9th | 25th | -16 |
| UK | 15th | 29th | -14 |
| Australia | 11th | 25th | -14 |
| United States | 16th | 23rd | -7 |
| New Zealand | 8th | 11th | -3 |

**Youth happiness divergence:** Among Americans, Australians, British, Canadians, Irish, and New Zealanders under 25, happiness scores "have fallen -- placing all six among the biggest declines for this age group."

**Canada age gap:** Older Canadians ranked 8th in happiness; younger Canadians dropped to 58th place.

**Contrasting pattern:** Nations with lower migration (Poland, Eastern European countries, Latin America, East Asia) maintained or increased youth happiness levels during the same period.

**Key quote from The Economist's analysis:** "Nearly twice as many countries have grown happier over the past two decades as have become less so. The English-speaking world is a clear exception."

### WHR 2018 (dedicated immigration chapter)
- Happiness rankings were "essentially the same" for native-born and foreign-born populations in each country
- Immigrant happiness converges close to native-born levels in destination countries
- The happiness gap between immigrants and natives in the same country is generally small

### WHR 2024
- Focused on happiness by age group/generation, not immigration specifically
- Documented sharp youth happiness declines in Anglosphere countries
