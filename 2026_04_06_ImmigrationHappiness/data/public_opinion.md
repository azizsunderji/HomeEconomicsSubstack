# Public Opinion on Immigration: Polling Data Across Countries

## Research date: April 6, 2026

---

## 1. UNITED STATES (Gallup, June 2025)

### "Should immigration be increased, decreased, or kept at present level?"

| Response | June 2025 | June 2024 | Change |
|----------|-----------|-----------|--------|
| Decreased | 30% | 55% | -25 pts |
| Present level | 38% | -- | -- |
| Increased | 26% | -- | -- |

This represents a dramatic reversal of a four-year trend (2021-2024) of rising concern about immigration.

### "Is immigration a good thing or bad thing for the country?"

| Response | 2025 | 2024 | Change |
|----------|------|------|--------|
| Good thing | 79% (record high) | 64% | +15 pts |
| Bad thing | 17% (record low) | 32% | -15 pts |

### Party Breakdown -- Want Immigration Decreased

| Party | 2025 | 2024 | Change |
|-------|------|------|--------|
| Republicans | 48% | 88% | -40 pts |
| Independents | 30% | 51% | -21 pts |
| Democrats | 16% | 28% | -12 pts |

### Party Breakdown -- Immigration Is Good for Country

| Party | 2025 |
|-------|------|
| Democrats | 91% (record high) |
| Independents | ~80% |
| Republicans | ~66% (up from 39% in 2024) |

### Policy Support (June 2025)

| Policy | Support |
|--------|---------|
| Pathway to citizenship for undocumented | 78% (up from 70%) |
| Pathway for childhood arrivals | 80%+ |
| Border Patrol expansion | 59% (down from 76%) |
| Border wall expansion | 45% (down 8 pts) |
| Mass deportation | 38% (down from 47%) |

### Hispanic American Views (June 2025)
- Support citizenship pathway: 91%
- Want immigration decreased: 39% (higher than national 30%)
- Support mass deportation: 23%
- Approve of Trump on immigration: 21%

### Immigration as "Most Important Problem" (Gallup)
- Named top problem for three consecutive months in early 2024
- Concern has since "abated" significantly by mid-2025

### Trump Immigration Approval (June 2025)
- National: 35%
- Republicans: 85%
- Independents: 28%
- Democrats: 2%

**Survey methodology:** 1,402 adults, June 2-26, 2025. Margin of error: +/-4 pts. Hispanic oversample: 300 adults, MOE +/-7 pts.

### Pew Research Findings (2024-2025)
- 59% approve of increasing deportation efforts (but views becoming more mixed over time)
- 53% say Trump administration doing "too much" on deportation (up from 44% in March 2025)
- 77% say undocumented immigrants mostly fill jobs US citizens don't want
- 56% of Republicans say all undocumented immigrants should be deported; 62% of Democrats say some should be
- Immigrant population declined from 53.3M (Jan 2025) to 51.9M (June 2025)

## 2. UNITED KINGDOM

### YouGov Tracker (December 2024)
- **70%** say immigration over the last 10 years has been "too high" (record high since tracker began 2019)
  - 50% say "much too high"
  - 15% say "about right"
  - 3% say "too low"

### Immigration "Good or Bad for Country" (YouGov)
- 43% say immigration over last decade has been "mostly bad for the country" (highest ever)
- 18% say "mostly good"
- In 2019 (start of tracker): 29% good vs. 29% bad (equal)

### Legal vs. Illegal Migration Distinction
- 79% say too much illegal migration
- 48% say too much legal migration

### Ipsos UK (2024)
- Two-thirds of Britons say total number of people entering UK is "too high"

### Best for Britain Survey
- Only 1 in 4 Britons worried about immigration specifically in their own community (vs. abstract national concern)

## 3. CANADA

### IRCC Telephone Tracking Survey (2024)
- **54%** say too many immigrants are coming to Canada
- Top reasons cited: lack of housing, lack of infrastructure and social services
- Described as "possibly the biggest shift in Canadian public opinion towards immigration in the last generation"

### Historical Context
- Canada historically ranked as world's most accepting country for migrants on Gallup's Migrant Acceptance Index (score: 8.46/9.0)
- Public opinion "soured" dramatically during 2022-2024 period of rapid population growth

## 4. AUSTRALIA

### Scanlon Foundation Mapping Social Cohesion (2024)

**"Is immigration too high?"**

| Year | Too High | About Right | Too Low |
|------|----------|-------------|---------|
| 2019 | 41% | -- | -- |
| 2023 | 33% | -- | -- |
| 2024 | 51% (record) | -- | -- |

- 18-point jump from 2023 to 2024 (33% to 51%) -- largest single-year increase in survey history
- Highest proportion ever recorded since survey began in 2007

**Social Cohesion Index:**
- 2024 score: 78 (lowest since survey began in 2007, same as 2023)
- Key pressures: economy, housing, immigration, safety

**Multiculturalism:**
- 85% agree multiculturalism is good for Australia
- 71% of those who say immigration is too high still support multiculturalism
- Concern driven by "economic and housing issues rather than attitudes to diversity, multiculturalism and migrants"

## 6. GLOBAL / CROSS-COUNTRY COMPARISONS

### Ipsos Global Trends 9th Edition (2025) -- 43 Markets
- **65%** of citizens globally say "there are too many immigrants in my country"
- Up 4 percentage points from previous survey
- Rising immigration concern linked to broader decline in optimism:
  - 59% optimistic about themselves/family (down 7 pts)
  - 45% optimistic about their city/town

### Gallup Migrant Acceptance Index (most recent available)
**Most accepting countries:**
1. Canada (8.46/9.0)
2. Iceland (8.41/9.0)
3. New Zealand

**Least accepting:** Hungary, Croatia, Latvia, Slovakia

**EU divide:** Western Europe index score: 6.73 vs. Central/Eastern Europe: 2.77

NOTE: This index predates the 2022-2024 immigration surge. More recent data from country-specific surveys (above) suggests acceptance has declined significantly in traditionally welcoming countries.

### Gallup World Poll -- Immigration and Happiness
- In the 2018 analysis, happiness rankings were "essentially the same" for native-born and foreign-born populations
- Immigrant happiness converges toward native-born levels in destination countries
- Exceptions: In Mexico, immigrants rank 10th for happiness while native-born rank 24th; in Switzerland and Netherlands, immigrant happiness ranks below natives

## 7. KEY PATTERNS ACROSS COUNTRIES

### The 2022-2024 Shift
All five English-speaking countries experienced a similar pattern:
1. Record or near-record immigration surges (2022-2023)
2. Sharp public opinion backlash (2023-2024)
3. Government policy reversals toward restriction (2024-2025)
4. Declining happiness rankings in World Happiness Report

### Immigration Concern Is Primarily Economic, Not Cultural
Multiple surveys across countries show:
- Australia: Concern "strongly related to economic and housing issues rather than attitudes to diversity"
- Canada: Top reasons for opposition are "lack of housing" and "lack of infrastructure"
- UK: Concerns center on public services, housing, and wages
- Even where people say immigration is "too high," large majorities still support multiculturalism/diversity

### The Paradox
- In the US, 79% say immigration is "good for the country" AND 30% want it decreased (these overlap)
- In Australia, 51% say immigration is too high AND 85% say multiculturalism is good
- People distinguish between immigration as a concept and the pace/scale of recent flows

### Immigration Attitudes and Happiness -- Direct Evidence
No major polling organization has directly surveyed the link between personal happiness/life satisfaction and immigration attitudes. The connection is inferred from:
- Parallel trends (declining happiness + rising immigration concern in same countries)
- The WHR 2026 data showing English-speaking high-immigration countries are outliers in happiness decline
- Ipsos finding that rising anti-immigrant sentiment correlates with declining personal optimism

DATA NEEDED: A direct survey question linking immigration attitudes to personal wellbeing/life satisfaction does not appear to exist in the major polling databases.
