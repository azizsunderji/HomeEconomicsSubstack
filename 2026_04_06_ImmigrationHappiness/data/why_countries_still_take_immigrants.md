# Why countries that prefer less immigration still take in many

_User-provided analysis, saved for article writing._

## Five mechanisms

1. **Legal and treaty obligations** — EU free movement, Geneva Convention, ECHR create binding floors. Even Orbán hasn't fully exited these systems.

2. **Labor market demand** — Agriculture, elder care, construction, hospitality have structural shortages. Employer lobbying quietly pushes back against the hardest restrictions. Danish employers lobby for skilled worker visas even as Social Democrats run on restriction.

3. **Family reunification** — Large share of inflows is spouses, children, parents of existing residents. Very hard to cut without measures (Denmark's "24-year rule") that generate backlash. Beneficiaries have citizen advocates.

4. **Right-wing parties prefer the issue to the solution** — Immigration as grievance is more electorally useful than immigration solved. Cas Mudde argues far-right parties have weak policy coherence on immigration because they've never had to govern the problem fully. When they do govern (Sweden Democrats, Danish People's Party in support roles), they accept compromises.

5. **Administrative and implementation capacity** — Deportation requires origin-country cooperation, legal processes, significant resources. Denmark deports a relatively high share and still finds many removal orders go unexecuted.

## Key insight

The stated preference of governing coalitions really is often lower immigration. But legal constraints, economic pressure, political incentives around issue ownership, and implementation limits create a floor substantially above zero even under restrictionist governments.

## Test case

Denmark under the Social Democrats — more aggressive than the right on restriction (paradigm shift policy, Rwanda plan attempt, ghetto laws) precisely to neutralize the issue rather than ride it. Different political logic than the right's.
